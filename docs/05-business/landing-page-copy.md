Here is our deal, in plain language.

The club's data belongs to the club. Not to us, not to advertisers, not to whoever pays the most. And every member controls exactly what the club sees, with switches they can flip anytime:

### 1.9 FAQ (homepage)

**1. Who owns our club's data?**
You do. The club owns its data; each member controls what the club sees through consent settings they manage themselves. We never sell data. Brands only ever see aggregated, anonymized insights — never groups smaller than 50 people — unless a member individually opts into a specific campaign. If you leave, you export everything.

**2. Does RunOS replace WhatsApp?**
Only if you want it to. RunOS has its own feed and messaging, and many clubs move announcements there so they stop getting buried. But we also integrate with WhatsApp, Slack, and Discord. Most clubs run both for a while, then notice the group chat has gone quiet in a good way — the noise moved, the friendships stayed.

**3. Do members have to pay for RunOS?**
No. Members never pay to use RunOS. The club subscribes; members get the app, their profile, the benefits passport, and consent controls free. Members only ever pay for things they'd pay for anyway — dues, event tickets, merch — set by the club, not by us.

**4. We have years of data in spreadsheets and Eventbrite. How does migration work?**
We import members, events, and payment history from spreadsheets, Eventbrite, Mailchimp exports, and most common tools. Starter and Club clubs use self-serve import with guided mapping. Founding clubs and Pro clubs get white-glove migration — we do it for you. Most clubs are fully live within a week.

**5. What happens if we cancel?**
No lock-in. Cancel anytime from settings. You get a full export of your data — members, events, finances, everything — in standard formats. Your Stripe account is yours, so payment history stays with you. We keep your export available for 90 days after cancellation, then delete your data on request or per our retention policy.

**6. How does the platform fee work?**
When money moves through RunOS — dues, tickets, merch — we take a small platform fee: 2% on Starter, 1% on Club, 0.5% on Pro. Paid registrations on Starter and Club also carry a 2% + $0.30 ticket fee. Stripe's standard processing fees apply separately, as they would with any tool. The more you grow, the smaller our slice.

**7. What is Pacer, exactly?**
Pacer is your club's digital COO — an AI assistant grounded in your club's own data. It predicts attendance, flags churn risk, drafts sponsor proposals, builds event landing pages, writes newsletters and Instagram carousels, and plans whole months on request. It never sees or exposes another club's raw data. Unlimited on Pro; $29/mo add-on for Starter and Club.

**8. Do our members' Strava runs get shared automatically?**
No. Members choose what syncs and what the club sees. By default the club sees summaries, not detailed routes or paces — and members can dial that up or down anytime. Medical details and live location are always opt-in. We support Strava, Garmin, COROS, Polar, Suunto, Apple Health, Google Health Connect, Fitbit, TrainingPeaks, and Zwift.

**9. We're volunteers with zero budget. Is Starter really free?**
Really free. Under 50 members, core Community + Events, no time limit, no credit card. You'll see RunOS branding and pay the 2% platform fee only when money actually moves. When you outgrow 50 members, you'll be earning enough from dues and events to make Club an easy call.

**10. Is RunOS only for road running clubs?**
RunOS is built for running communities first — road, trail, track, triathlon crews, and university clubs. If your community meets, moves, and organizes events, the operating system fits. Multi-sport and franchise fitness communities run on our Network tier.

### 2.2 The problem: spreadsheet-and-hope sponsorship

**Headline:** You know the drill. Money out, mystery back.

You sponsor a run club. You wire the money, ship the banners, maybe fund a race kit. Then the reporting arrives: a few Instagram stories, a claimed member count nobody can verify, and a thank-you email. Was it 400 active runners or 400 names on a dead list from 2022? Did anyone actually try the shoe? You genuinely cannot know.

So sponsorship stays a "brand" line item — the budget that gets cut first, defended with vibes instead of numbers. Meanwhile the audience you want most, committed runners who show up weekly and influence their whole crew, keeps meeting every Saturday, unmeasured.

### 2.3 The verified-audience value prop

**Headline:** Verified communities. Not claimed followers.

Every club on RunOS runs its operations through the platform — events, check-ins, payments, activity sync. That means the numbers you see are operational facts, not media-kit fiction:

- **Verified membership** — actual paying, active members, not list size.
- **Verified attendance** — QR check-ins at real events, week after week.
- **Verified activity** — training volume synced from Strava, Garmin, and eight other sources, aggregated and anonymized.
- **Verified redemptions** — when members claim your perk, you see it.

You're not buying impressions. You're reaching the highest-intent running audience that exists: people who lace up at 7 a.m. with their club — and everyone who asks them what shoes to buy.

### 2.4 How campaigns work

**Headline:** Consent-first. Aggregate by default. Effective anyway.

1. **Explore anonymized audiences.** Browse clubs and segments by city, size, activity level, and engagement — all aggregated and anonymized. No segment smaller than 50 members is ever shown, so no individual is ever identifiable.
2. **Propose a campaign.** Perks in the benefits passport, product demos at events, sponsored challenges, sampling activations. Clubs accept, negotiate, or decline — they hold the keys to their community.
3. **Members opt in individually.** Only members who explicitly turn on brand marketing hear from you at all. When someone opts into your specific campaign, you get a genuinely interested runner — the opposite of an ignored ad.
4. **Measure everything.** Redemptions, event attendance, challenge completions, repeat engagement. One dashboard, exportable, defensible in any budget review.

This isn't a limitation. It's why it works. An audience that chose to hear from you outperforms an audience that couldn't escape you.

- The live spot counter on this page is the source of truth. When it hits zero, it's zero.

### 6.2 A/B test plan — hero variants

**Test 1: H1 vs H3 (control vs problem-first challenger)**
- **Hypothesis:** The problem-first headline (H3, "Your club runs on 7 apps and one exhausted volunteer. Fix both.") will lift signup starts by at least 15% relative over H1 for cold traffic, because organizers self-identify with the named pain faster than with a promise.
- **Primary metric:** Free-signup start rate (click on primary CTA / unique homepage visitors). Secondary: signup completion rate, demo-video plays.
- **Sample size guidance:** Assuming a 4% baseline CTA click rate and a 15% relative minimum detectable effect at 80% power and 95% confidence, plan for roughly 23,000 visitors per variant (~46,000 total). At early-stage traffic, expect 6–10 weeks; if traffic is below ~5,000/month, don't run this test — pick H1 on judgment and revisit at scale.
- **Run rules:** 50/50 split, new visitors only, no mid-test copy edits to any other homepage section, minimum 2 full weeks even if significance appears early (weekday/weekend cycles matter for club organizers), stop only at predetermined sample size or 8 weeks. One test at a time on the homepage.

**Test 2 (after a winner): winner vs H5 (identity-shift)**
- **Hypothesis:** For retargeting and email-referred traffic, the identity headline (H5, "Stop being the club's unpaid admin. Start being its leader.") will beat the Test 1 winner on signup completion (not just clicks), because warm visitors need a reason to act now, not a category explanation.
- **Primary metric:** Signup completion rate. Guardrail: bounce rate must not rise more than 10% relative.
- **Run rules:** Same as Test 1; segment analysis by traffic source is pre-registered (cold search vs retargeting vs referral), since we expect H5 to win warm and lose cold.

**Standing rules for all hero tests:**
- Never test headline and subhead simultaneously; isolate one variable.
- Log every test (hypothesis, dates, sample, result) in the growth log before launch.
- A losing test that teaches us which pain resonates is a win. Ship the learning into ad copy and email subject lines within a week.
